A five-minute drawing icebreaker for a team meeting

Ahmed Moaz · 11 November 2026

A meeting that starts with a game starts better. A drawing game is good for it, because nobody has to be good, and the worst pictures get the biggest laugh. This is a five-minute version.

The settings

Open a table and set two rounds. Choose a drawing time of 40 seconds. Leave three words to pick from and three hints. If you have eight people, that is 16 drawings, at most 11 minutes. With four people it is 8 drawings, at most 5 minutes. Most drawings end sooner.

Your own words

The host may type a hidden list of words for the table. For a team, use words from the week: a project name, a place, a tool, a joke. It turns an icebreaker into a small review.

How to run it

  1. Send the link before the meeting starts. A guest needs only a name.
  2. Join early. People can come in while others are still arriving, and a latecomer can join a game that has started.
  3. Ask people to guess in the chat. Right guesses are hidden, so nobody gets the answer for free.
  4. Look at the gallery. The recap lays out every drawing from the game.

Why it works for a team

The scoring rewards speed, not skill, so a manager and an intern are on the same footing. A rough sketch that gets guessed fast scores more than a careful drawing that takes the whole clock.

What to leave out

Do not keep score for a prize. Let it be a warm-up. If someone wants to carry on, play again after the meeting.

Where to play

A meeting of up to twelve fits Boardit's Draw and Guess.

What to do if the room is split

A mix of people in a room and on a call is fine, since each person plays on their own screen. Share the link in the call chat and let people join from whatever device they have. Put the call on mute during a drawing so the answers stay in the chat, where right guesses are hidden.

One small group

The built-in words are organised in twenty groups, from objects and food down to the smallest, the landmarks group with only 11 words. For a team meeting, none of that matters: a table of colleagues will meet ordinary objects and animals, which are the biggest groups.

The meeting rule

Draw, laugh, then work.
