A teen birthday drawing game with the guests' names as words

Ahmed Moaz · 12 October 2026

A birthday party for teenagers can run on an online drawing game where the words are the guests' names, with everyone playing with friends on their own phones. It only works if the word list is built for it. Put ten names into Draw and Guess's normal word list and, by my arithmetic, a name turns up in about one game in five. The fix is a custom list used on its own. This is a plan for eight guests aged 13 or over with an adult in charge, with heats, snacks and a final, and with the timings worked out so the cake is not late.

Why names alone, not names mixed in

The game's built-in list holds about 1,600 words, by my count of the word file on 10 Oct 2026. A table of four playing three rounds is 12 turns, and each turn offers the drawer three words, so 36 words are dealt. With 10 names mixed in among roughly 1,640, each dealt word has about a 0.6% chance of being a name. Over 36 words that comes to about 20%.

So the host should switch on custom words only. That needs ten or more different words, and the list stays hidden from the rest of the table. The drawer is offered a few words at a time, and every word offered counts as used, not only the one picked. Two offers a turn is enough for a party.

The plan: heats, snacks, final

Eight guests split into two tables of four. Each table needs its own host, because one account can sit at only one table at a time, so two guests who have played before open one table each. Both switch their table to private. New tables are public by default, which lists them in Open tables, where a stranger could take a seat.

Heats use custom words only, with 2 rounds and two words offered. Four players for two rounds is 8 turns, and 8 turns at two offers is 16 words. So the list needs 16 words: the eight first names, plus eight things only this group knows. Every word is then offered once.

Not every name gets drawn, because the drawer picks one of two. A turn takes at most 12 seconds to choose, 80 to draw and 6 for the result, which is 98 seconds. Eight turns is 13 minutes at the longest, and both tables play at once.

After the heats, take 10 minutes for snacks and keep the food away from the devices.

The top two from each heat go to the final table of four, and the other four play a consolation table. Both use the plain settings: 3 rounds, 80 seconds, the built-in words. That is 12 turns, 19.6 minutes at the longest. In all, the longest plan is about 33 minutes of play. If two players are level for second, let both through and raise the table limit to six.

A rough running order for the party, with the longest times:

  • 0:00 to 0:10: seat the guests and open the tables;
  • 0:10 to 0:23: the heats;
  • 0:23 to 0:33: snacks;
  • 0:33 to 0:53: the final and the consolation table at once;
  • then cake.

Ground rules for names

Ask each guest first, and let anyone strike their own name from the list. A name can sting, and a veto costs nothing.

Some details about how names are judged:

  • Capitals and accents do not matter, and a hyphen counts as a space.
  • A guess that contains the whole name, such as "is it Sam?", counts as right.
  • A single-letter typo gets a private "close" notice, but only on words of four letters or longer, so a three-letter name gets no near-miss.
  • The drawer cannot write the name on the page.

Who it is for

This plan is for guests aged 13 or older. Boardit is not made for younger children, and I would not run this party for them. Guests need only a name to join, with no account, and the adult is in the room from the start.

Put the cake on the table when the final starts, not before.
