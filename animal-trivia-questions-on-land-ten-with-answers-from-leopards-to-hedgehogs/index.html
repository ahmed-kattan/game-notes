Animal trivia questions on land: ten with answers, from leopards to hedgehogs

Ahmed Moaz · 2 November 2026

These animal trivia questions stay on dry land. There are ten, and each answer carries one extra fact, so whoever reads them out has something to say after the reveal. I pulled them from the question set behind my own game, and every answer has a reference page behind it, listed in the front matter. Nothing here needs a map, a zoo visit or a wildlife film. If you can picture a camel, you can start a trivia quiz with this list.

Ten questions, easiest first

  1. Which spotted big cat drags its prey up a tree so that lions cannot take it?
  2. What does a camel store in its hump?
  3. Which fast cat can purr but cannot fully roar, and chirps to call its cubs?
  4. Where are a tiger's stripes, besides on its fur?
  5. Which African animal is known for a call that sounds like laughter?
  6. Which land mammal is pregnant for about 22 months?
  7. Which Australian animal has fingerprints so like ours that they could confuse a crime scene?
  8. Which North American runner can hold about 55 kilometres an hour for several kilometres?
  9. Which long-snouted South American animal flicks a sticky tongue to catch insects?
  10. Which spiny animal rolls into a ball when it feels threatened?

The answers

  1. A leopard. It hauls the kill into the branches, which is why you often see leopards resting in trees.
  2. Fat, not water. The hump is an energy store, and that is why a camel copes so well without a drink.
  3. A cheetah. It is built for speed, not for roaring.
  4. On its skin. The pattern runs deeper than the fur, and no two tigers carry the same one.
  5. The spotted hyena. It is also a skilled hunter, not only a scavenger.
  6. The elephant. A newborn calf weighs about 100 kilograms.
  7. The koala. Its fingerprints are very close to a human's.
  8. The pronghorn. The cheetah sprints faster, but the pronghorn keeps up its speed for far longer.
  9. The giant anteater, which can flick its tongue about 150 times a minute.
  10. The hedgehog, which has roughly 5,000 to 7,000 spines.

Which of these questions trip people up?

Question 2 catches most tables, because the water story is so well known. Question 8 catches the sports fans, who say cheetah without thinking. The cheetah wins a short sprint, so the question turns on the words "several kilometres". If you read it aloud, lean on them a little.

Question 3 also splits a room. Plenty of people expect every big cat to roar, and the cheetah is the one that does not quite manage it. A good host lets the table argue for ten seconds before giving the answer.

What order works for a round of ten?

Open with 2, 4 and 10, which most players can reason out. Put 1, 3, 5 and 9 in the middle. Keep 6, 7 and 8 for the end, because they ask for a number, a surprising match or a place that only a keen animal fan would know. For a longer trivia night, slot the set in after a round of general knowledge, when the room is warm and ready for something lighter.

If two players finish level, ask how many metres across a moose's antlers can be. Whoever lands closer takes it. The answer is about 1.8.

Can you play these online with friends?

Boardit's Trivia Tavern lets the host choose Science and nature, or keep the whole game on Mixed, and every player answers on a personal screen. It starts as a race. Everybody answers, each right answer earns points, and the quickest correct player earns the most. If the table has young players, one tap slows the pace. It also works as online trivia for people in different cities, since each person joins from a browser.
