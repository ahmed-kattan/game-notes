Brand and logo trivia questions: 10 on the stories behind famous names

Ahmed Moaz · 20 October 2026

Every famous logo has a small, odd story. These ten brand and logo trivia questions cover a swoosh, a prancing horse and a plastic brick, each answered with a note and checked against a named source. Use them at a party, in a classroom or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. Which brand's logo is a swoosh?
  2. Which car company has a prancing horse as its logo?
  3. Which car company's logo is a raging bull?
  4. What is the name of the toy brand known for its plastic building bricks?
  5. Which company makes the iPhone?
  6. Which fast-food chain has golden arches as its logo?
  7. What is the name of the social network founded by Mark Zuckerberg?
  8. Which company created the Windows operating system?
  9. Which company owns YouTube?
  10. Which famous puzzle has six coloured faces you twist to solve?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Nike. The swoosh was designed in 1971 by a student, Carolyn Davidson, who was paid 35 dollars.
  2. Ferrari. The prancing horse was painted on the plane of an Italian First World War flying ace.
  3. Lamborghini. Ferruccio Lamborghini was a Taurus, and many of his cars are named after fighting bulls.
  4. Lego, from the Danish leg godt, meaning play well.
  5. Apple. The first iPhone was unveiled by Steve Jobs in January 2007.
  6. McDonald's. The first one opened in San Bernardino, California, in 1940.
  7. Facebook, which began in 2004 as a site for Harvard students only.
  8. Microsoft, founded by Bill Gates and Paul Allen in 1975.
  9. Google, which bought YouTube in 2006 for 1.65 billion dollars, a year after it launched.
  10. The Rubik's Cube. Its Hungarian inventor took about a month to solve it.

How hard is the set?

Questions 1 to 5 are for everyone. Questions 6 to 8 are medium. Questions 9 and 10 are fairly easy but have notes that surprise. The star of the set is the first, because a famous logo cost its designer so little.

How do you use it on the night?

As a trivia night closer, this set is light and quick: the answers are known by almost everyone, and the notes are the real prize of the round.

Why does the 35 dollar swoosh matter?

Because it shows that value and cost are different things. A student was paid an amount that would not buy a pair of the shoes it now decorates. A quizmaster who pauses on that fact gets the table talking about design, luck and fairness. It is the strongest kind of trivia: a number that starts a debate.

How do you make it a design round?

Show each logo on a screen with the name hidden, and ask the table to name it before you ask the question. Then ask the team to draw a logo for an imaginary brand in thirty seconds. The results are always funnier than the originals, and the table learns how hard a good mark is to draw.

What are good follow-ups?

Ask who designed the Apple logo, what colour the Coca-Cola can is and which brand uses a mermaid. They are not in this set, but the table that enjoyed it will have ideas of its own. A round where each team writes one brand question for the others is a fine second half.
