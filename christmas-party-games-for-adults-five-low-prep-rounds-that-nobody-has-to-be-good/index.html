Christmas party games for adults: five low-prep rounds that nobody has to be good at

Ahmed Moaz · 3 November 2026

Adults at a party fall into two camps. Some want a game with rules and a winner. Others want an excuse to talk and a drink in their hand. Good Christmas party games for grown-ups work for both, take ten minutes to explain and ask nobody to sing or act. Here are five, in my running order. Four are party games online, so they also work when guests are spread out.

Round one: the white elephant swap

Start with a gift exchange, because it needs no screens and it warms a room. It is known as white elephant, Yankee swap or Dirty Santa, depending on where you live.

Everyone brings one wrapped gift of about the same value. Draw numbers to set the order. On your turn you either open a new gift or steal one that has already been opened. If yours is stolen, you open another or steal in turn. A gift can be stolen only a set number of times, commonly twice, and then its holder keeps it. The game ends when everyone holds a gift.

A tip for a mixed group: set the price cap low, and ask for gifts that are useful or funny, not both. Nothing sours a swap like an expensive gift in a pile of chocolates.

Round two: Who's the Spy for a talkative table

I make Boardit, which offers six free multiplayer browser games without a download. Who's the Spy suits a table of adults who like to argue.

All but one player see the same secret word; the spy sees only the topic. In turn, each person types a short clue of 20 characters or fewer, and the table then votes on who does not belong. There are 25 themed word packs, from Food and Travel to Music and Celebrations & events, and the host can also type a private list.

Two features matter with grown-ups. First, the spy draw is fair: the person who was the spy last round is not picked again unless the group is tiny, and each round you have already been the spy makes it less likely you will be chosen. Nobody can claim the host rigged it. Second, the host can turn on an Undercover player, who is given a similar word and does not know they are the odd one out. That produces the funniest moment of the night, when someone confidently describes the wrong thing.

It takes three to eight online players, about 20 minutes.

Round three: a drawing game for people who swear they cannot draw

Drawing is a good equaliser. Nobody is good at drawing in 60 seconds, so everyone laughs at the same level. The host can choose words from a pack or type their own list, and every drawing is saved in a gallery at the end, so you can pass the phone around and scroll through the disasters.

Keep the words within the room. A list about the year's shared jokes works well, and a list about people does not.

Round four: Trivia with a pub-quiz mood

Trivia Tavern covers nine categories, or Mixed. Pick two or three for the night, such as Music and Entertainment, and keep it to one game. The host can make it gentle. A slow pace suits a room where half the people are standing at the buffet.

Round five: a sleepy finish

End with Snakes and Ladders, which takes up to eight players, or Ludo for up to four. Both are luck-heavy, so a lost game stings no one. It is the board-game equivalent of a slow song once the dancing is done.

Which rounds work for a mixed group?

If your table has guests who have never met, start with the swap and the drawing game. Both break the ice without asking anyone to be clever. Keep Who's the Spy for a group that already trusts each other, since it works by bluffing.

If half the guests are on a call, add the browser games: three to eight at the table for Spy, up to twelve for drawing. A remote guest needs only a name to sit down.

How do you keep adult games from going too far?

Set one rule before you start: no one is the butt of the joke. A group that agrees on this in the first minute plays better for the next two hours. Offer a "pass" on any turn, and take it yourself once, so others feel free to use it.

Finally, end at a sensible time. A game night that stops while everyone is still smiling gets repeated next year.
