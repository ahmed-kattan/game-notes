City trivia questions: which country is each of these 10 cities in?

Ahmed Moaz · 22 October 2026

A famous city name often leaves its country just out of reach. These ten city trivia questions ask which country each one is in, with a note on what makes it famous. Use them at a table or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends who travel.

The questions

  1. Which country is the city of Istanbul in?
  2. Which country is the city of Rio de Janeiro in?
  3. Which country is the city of Marrakesh in?
  4. Which country is the city of Munich in?
  5. Which country is the city of Kyoto in?
  6. In which country is the city of Mecca?
  7. In which country is the city of Cape Town?
  8. In which country is the city of Lagos?
  9. Which country is the city of Sydney in?
  10. Which country is the city of Toronto in?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Turkey. Istanbul sits on two continents, Europe and Asia.
  2. Brazil. Rio was Brazil's capital until Brasília was built in 1960.
  3. Morocco. Its main square, Jemaa el-Fnaa, fills with snake charmers and food stalls at night.
  4. Germany. Munich hosts Oktoberfest, the world's biggest beer festival.
  5. Japan. Kyoto has about 1,600 Buddhist temples.
  6. Saudi Arabia. Millions of Muslims travel to Mecca every year for the Hajj pilgrimage.
  7. South Africa. Cape Town sits beneath the flat-topped Table Mountain.
  8. Nigeria. Lagos is one of the fastest-growing cities in the world.
  9. Australia. Sydney Harbour Bridge is nicknamed the Coathanger.
  10. Canada. The CN Tower was the world's tallest freestanding structure for 32 years.

How hard is the set?

The set is gentle: questions 1 to 6 are familiar names, and questions 7 to 10 are the ones that might trip a table that has not travelled. For children, show a photo of each city after the answer.

How do you use it on the night?

As a trivia game for a long journey, ask the questions in the order of the route and let each passenger take a turn as quizmaster.

What do the notes add?

A picture of the city in one sentence. Kyoto's temples, Munich's festival and Rio's old capital status give each place a character. A table that knows only the name will leave with a reason to care. That is the strongest thing a geography quiz can do.

How do you widen the round?

Ask for the nearest ocean, the language and the currency, as bonus questions for each city. Or turn it around: read the country and ask for its most famous city. A table that enjoys the first ten will have fun with a reverse round, and the second round is always faster than the first.

What makes a city famous?

A single detail, usually. A square at night, a festival, a tall tower, a sacred pilgrimage. The questions ask for the country, but the notes give the detail, and the detail is what a traveller remembers. A table that has been to some of these places will have its own stories, and the strongest evenings are those where the quiz is just the first line.

A note on place names

Place names change with politics and time, as Saigon and Ho Chi Minh City did. Where the bank uses a current name, I have followed it, and a quizmaster should accept a former name as a half point.
