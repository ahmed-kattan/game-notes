Custom words for a draw and guess game: how to write a list your group will love

Ahmed Moaz · 6 November 2026

A draw and guess game is good with its standard words. It is excellent with yours. When a table can add its own words, it can make jokes only the group understands, honour a birthday or train a class on its vocabulary. But a bad custom list is worse than none. Here is how to write one that works.

Why add your own words?

Because shared memory is the richest fuel for a game. A drawing of "the time we got lost in the car park" gets a roar from the people who were there and a blank from everyone else. A table of friends or family has dozens of such references, and the game turns them into points.

It also lets you control difficulty. A children's party can use simple words. A work team can use terms from the job. A class can use the week's vocabulary.

How many words do you need?

Enough that no word repeats in an evening. A table of six on three rounds plays 18 drawings. Because the drawer chooses from several offered words, you want at least three times that: 50 words is a good start. For a larger table or more rounds, aim for 100.

What makes a good custom word?

  1. It has a picture, or a symbol the group knows.
  2. It is shared: most players should know it.
  3. It is specific enough to be funny.
  4. It is not so obscure that nobody can guess.

A word the whole table knows but nobody can easily draw is perfect.

What makes a bad one?

  • Anything that needs written text to explain.
  • Anything that embarrasses a person at the table.
  • Anything that only one person in the group understands.
  • Anything abstract with no symbol, such as "optimism".

How do you organise a list?

Group the words by theme, so you can balance a game. For a family evening: places we have been, foods we love, family nicknames, family sayings. For a team: tools, tasks, meetings, the office kitchen. For a class: this week's vocabulary, last week's, a few revision words.

How do you balance difficulty?

Mix three levels. Easy words get the table started and give weaker drawers a win. Medium words make up most of the game. A few hard words add spice. A ratio of two easy, five medium and one hard keeps most groups happy.

What kinds of lists work for special occasions?

For a birthday, collect words from the guest of honour's life: pets, hobbies, favourite foods and the places they have lived. For a wedding, the couple's history works, as long as the guests know it. For a leaving party, use the jokes of the office or the team. For a holiday trip, use the places and mishaps of the journey. Each list takes ten minutes to write and pays back in an evening of in-jokes.

Keep a private backup list of safe, general words in case a custom word falls flat. A host who can swap in a standard word keeps the game moving without fuss.

How do you add them?

The host adds them in the lobby before the game starts. On Board It, a free site I make, the table lets you add your own words, and then plays the game with friends in a browser. It is a free online drawing game for two to twelve, a party game that needs no download.

Can you share a list?

Yes. Keep it in a note on your phone and paste it each time. Some groups build a running list across evenings, adding five new words after every game. After a few months it is a diary of the group's jokes.

A last test

Before the party, read the list to one person who is not in on the plan. If they smile at half of it, you have a good list.
