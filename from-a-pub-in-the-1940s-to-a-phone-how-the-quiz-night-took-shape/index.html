From a pub in the 1940s to a phone: how the quiz night took shape

Ahmed Moaz · 10 November 2026

A trivia night feels like a modern idea, but it has a history. These facts come from Wikipedia's articles on pub quizzes and Trivial Pursuit, which I read on 17 October 2026.

The pub quiz

The pub quiz is usually traced to Britain. The Wikipedia article quotes a 1946 mention in the Guinness records, and says that leagues of pub quizzes grew up from the late 1950s. The format reached the United States in the 1990s.

The shape of a pub quiz has stayed the same for decades: teams, rounds on different topics, a host who reads the questions and an answer sheet. The social part, a table of people arguing, is the point.

Trivial Pursuit

The board game was created on 15 December 1979 in Montreal and released in 1981. By 2014 it had sold over 100 million copies, according to the same sources. It put the quiz in a box and made a night of it for families.

What a night needs

Looking at both, a good trivia night has four things:

  1. Questions that most people can try. Nobody enjoys a night of questions that only one person can answer.
  2. Rounds that build. A start that is easy, a middle that is harder, an end that matters.
  3. A table that talks. The argument is half the fun.
  4. A host who keeps time. A pub quiz needs someone to read the answers.

What an online quiz changes

You no longer need a room or a pen. Everyone answers on their own screen, and the scores are added up for you. The argument and the host remain. On Boardit, each round of Trivia Tavern moves from easy questions to a number question, and the last round counts double.

Why the format lasted

A quiz night needs almost nothing: some questions, some people and a way to keep score. That is why it survived from a pub in the 1940s to a board game in 1979 and a browser tab now. The parts that matter, the argument and the laugh, do not need technology.

The bank behind a modern quiz

The old pub quiz had a quizmaster with a folder. A modern one has a bank. Trivia Tavern draws from more than 2,500 questions, including 275 music questions, 248 history questions and 187 video games questions, so a night on a narrow subject still has a pool to draw on.

The line from then to now

A 1946 pub, a 1979 board game and a phone in your hand. The format bends, and the evening survives.
