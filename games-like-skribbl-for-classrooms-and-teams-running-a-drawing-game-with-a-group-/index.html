Games like skribbl for classrooms and teams: running a drawing game with a group of twenty

Ahmed Moaz · 30 October 2026

Few party games work in a classroom, a training day or a team call without an explanation, and a drawing game is one of them. Everyone knows what a pencil does. When teachers and team leads search for games like skribbl, they usually want something they can run for twenty minutes, with a group bigger than a living room. Below is a plan for doing that, with the numbers worked out. I build Boardit alone, and Draw and Guess works as an online drawing game, built for playing with friends, so I know its limits well. skribbl belongs to someone else; I have no tie to it.

How do you fit twenty people into a table of twelve?

You split them. One room seats two to twelve players, so a group of twenty becomes two rooms of ten. Another way is three rooms of seven or so if you want smaller teams and a bit of competition between them.

Appoint one host for each room. The host sets the clock, picks the words and starts the game. Everyone else only needs a link and a name. Nobody has to make an account, which matters on a school device or a work laptop where signing up is a chore.

I make a free drawing game called Boardit's Draw and Guess, and these plans are built around its settings.

What do the settings mean for a short session?

Two numbers set the length: how many players are in a room, and how many rounds you play. Each player gets one turn at the pencil per round. So the number of drawings is players times rounds.

These three plans are my calculation, each showing the most drawing time a session can take:

  • Ten players, two rounds: 20 drawings. At 60 seconds each, that is 20 minutes of drawing at most.
  • Six players, three rounds: 18 drawings. At 40 seconds each, 12 minutes.
  • Eight players, three rounds, the defaults: 24 drawings of up to 100 seconds, so 40 minutes at most.

Most drawings end sooner, because the turn closes when everyone has guessed. Word choice and results add a little on top. If you have a 45-minute lesson, the middle plan leaves room for a talk before and after.

Can you use your own words?

Yes, and for a class this is the whole point. The host types a list of their own words, and the players never see it in advance. A language teacher can load the week's vocabulary. A science teacher can use terms from the unit. A team lead can use project names and product features.

There are also word packs if you want a theme and do not have time to write a list. The drawer sees five words by default and picks one, so a hard word never traps anyone. The host can lower that number to two for a smaller choice.

How do hints help a mixed group?

The dashes show how long each word is, and a hint turns one of them into a letter as time drains away. The host chooses none, one, two or three. For a class I would leave all three on. They stop a drawing from ending in silence, and a student who is not confident at drawing still gets a turn that works.

Who is it suitable for?

The site is meant for people aged 13 and over. I would use this with older students, adults and teams, and leave younger children to a service built for them.

What keeps a classroom drawing game calm?

  • Say the rule out loud: the drawer may not write letters, and guesses go in the chat.
  • Pick each room's host before anyone sits down.
  • Decide in advance what to do with silly guesses. I would let them through. They are half the fun.
  • Run a short two-round trial with three or four players beforehand, so the real session starts smoothly.

What do you do with the drawings afterwards?

When the last round ends, every drawing from the game sits in a gallery. In a class this is a free review sheet. Go through the pictures and ask the group to say, without looking at the label, what each one was. In a team it is a laugh and an easy way to close the session.

A short checklist for the host

Count your players, divide them into rooms of up to twelve, choose the rounds and the clock from the plan above, write ten or so of your own words, and test the link on one device before the group arrives. After that the game runs itself, and you can watch the table.
