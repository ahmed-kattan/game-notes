Games to play on video call with a big family: household teams and one clear host

Ahmed Moaz · 27 October 2026

A big family on one call is a lovely idea and a noisy one. Twenty faces, three generations and four different internet connections. Games to play on video call in that situation need a structure, or they dissolve into everyone talking at once. This guide suggests a structure: count households, not heads, and give one person the job of host. Then it matches the games to the crowd.

Why count households, not people?

On a family call, each screen usually holds a household. A family of five behind one tablet is one participant, not five. If you pick games by the number of people, you will think your crowd is too big. If you pick by screens, a call of twenty people might be only seven or eight participants, and most games fit that.

So ask each household to share one device for the game. One person plays, the rest advise and laugh.

What does the host do?

Choose one person, and make their role clear. Their jobs are to choose the game, send the link ahead of time, mutes people who forget, watches the clock and ends the call. They do not play unless there is a spare seat. Hosting is a job, and a family call with no host will drift.

The host should also be the one person who deals with technology problems. Everyone else can ignore a stuck link and wait.

Which games stretch to a big family?

  • Draw and Guess takes up to twelve people. Everyone sees the drawing at once and guesses together. A relative who arrives late can still slip into a game that has started.
  • Snakes and Ladders has eight seats, and it needs no skill, so grandparents and toddlers are equal.
  • Trivia Tavern takes eight as well. It starts in race scoring, but the host can switch to a gentler pace for older relatives.
  • Who's the Spy seats three to eight online. It suits teenagers and adults, and the clue turns are short.
  • Ludo seats only four, so it works as a household-versus-household final, with teams of any size behind each seat.

How do you include relatives who are not on the same app?

Everyone can use a different call program, since the game runs in a browser. Apple's support page says anyone can join a FaceTime call from a web browser, including on Android or Windows, so even relatives with older phones can usually get in. For the game itself, a link in a family chat is all they need.

A sixty-minute running order

  1. Ten minutes of greetings. Let everyone talk, because that is what they came for.
  2. Fifteen minutes of Trivia Tavern, one team per household.
  3. Twenty-five minutes of Draw and Guess, with the host adding family words, such as the name of a pet or a place everyone knows.
  4. Ten minutes of Snakes and Ladders or a quiet closer.

How can the youngest and the oldest both join in?

Give the youngest the drawing job and the oldest the guessing job. In Draw and Guess, set the hints to the highest value of three, and the time to 120 seconds, so nobody is rushed. Let the children pick a word from the choices. Everyone has a role, and a role is what keeps a child from wandering off.

When the call has to end

Set a time and say it twice: once at the start, once five minutes before. Big family calls stretch like elastic. End the game before the energy drops, and save the final five minutes for goodbyes. Pick the date for the next game before everyone logs off, since a family game night held once a month is easier to protect than one that depends on whoever has a free evening.
