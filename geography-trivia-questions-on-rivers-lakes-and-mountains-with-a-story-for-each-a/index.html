Geography trivia questions on rivers, lakes and mountains, with a story for each answer

Ahmed Moaz · 28 October 2026

Rivers and mountains make good geography trivia questions because every answer has a story attached. Below are ten questions, then the answers with the one-line story I would read out after each. You can use them at a kitchen table or as one round of a trivia quiz you play online with friends.

The ten questions

  1. Which river flows through Paris?
  2. Which holy river runs past Varanasi, where pilgrims bathe?
  3. Which river runs through the city of Baghdad?
  4. Which river runs through Rome?
  5. What is the largest lake in Africa?
  6. Which mountain range holds Mount Everest?
  7. Which range crosses Switzerland, Austria and the north of Italy?
  8. Which mountain is the highest in Western Europe?
  9. Which range divides Europe from Asia across Russia?
  10. Everest sits right on a border. Which two countries share it?

The answers, with the story I would tell

  1. The Seine. About 37 bridges cross it inside Paris.
  2. The Ganges. Millions of people bathe in it during the Kumbh Mela festival.
  3. The Tigris. With the Euphrates, it gave Mesopotamia its name, "the land between the rivers".
  4. The Tiber. Legend says the twins Romulus and Remus were left in a basket on it.
  5. Lake Victoria. It is one of the sources of the Nile.
  6. The Himalayas. The name means "abode of snow" in Sanskrit.
  7. The Alps.
  8. Mont Blanc, at about 4,800 metres. It is also the top of the Alps.
  9. The Urals, which stretch about 2,500 kilometres from north to south.
  10. Nepal and China. The summit sits on the line between Nepal and Tibet.

Which of these will people miss?

Questions 1, 2 and 6 are easy and most tables get them. Questions 3, 4 and 5 are medium, because people know the cities but not the rivers beside them. Questions 8, 9 and 10 are where a table splits. Almost everyone knows Everest is in the Himalayas. Far fewer remember that its summit is a border.

I would play them in that order, easy first, so the table warms up. Boardit's trivia rounds run the same way, from easy questions to hard ones, because a hard question lands better after two quick wins.

How do you run it without a map?

Read each question once, slowly, then count to ten before you take answers. The city clue in questions 2, 3 and 4 helps people who do not know the river, so do not repeat it. If your group is large, write answers on paper and reveal them together, so nobody copies.

A map can come out afterwards. Point to the Tigris after question 3 and to the Urals after question 9, and the answers stick much better than a list of names would.

Where do these fit in a trivia night?

Geography works well as one round in a longer trivia night, next to general knowledge, music and sport. Ten questions is about the right size for a round. It lasts a few minutes, and nobody has time to get bored or to look anything up.

If you want a second round, write four more in the same style: another two rivers, another two ranges. Pick ones with a clear single answer and a fact you can tell afterwards.
