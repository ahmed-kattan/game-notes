Imposter game words for adults: 40 ideas that make a table sweat

Ahmed Moaz · 24 October 2026

Adults play this differently from children. They bluff more, accuse more and overthink more. The right words reward that. Good imposter game words for adults are specific enough to be tricky to describe, familiar enough that everyone has an opinion, and rich enough that a vague clue sounds suspicious. Here are 40, in four categories, for a kitchen table, a dinner party or a spy game online with friends.

What makes a word hard for adults?

Adults are good at spotting vague answers, so the strongest words are those where a vague clue gives you away. They need to have a distinct personality: a place with a smell, a job with a stereotype, an object with a habit. When the word has a personality, a player who has not heard it will struggle to fake it.

How do you give a clue with these?

Think of one detail only people who have been there would know. For "slow cooker", "all day" works, but "cheap" does not, because it fits half the household. For "night bus", "last one" is specific; "transport" is not. A good clue sounds as though it comes from a memory.

How do you catch a bluffer?

Listen for the clue that arrives in the voice of a guess. Real clues are specific and slightly odd. A bluffed clue is generic and cautious. If a player's two clues could fit the same ten words, they are probably the spy.

What about the spy's final guess?

In the game I run, a caught spy picks the word from ten options, so a lucky guess is right one time in ten. With adult words, the decoys are usually close, so the spy needs to have listened. It is a satisfying twist for the table to watch.

How many words do you need for an evening?

A table of six playing three rounds uses three words. But if you play several games, you will want a fresh word for every round, and not to repeat one within a night. Forty words in a bowl give you thirteen games, which is more than most evenings. Draw at random, fold the slips and let each person take one. The spy draws a slip that says only the category.

It helps to mark each slip with its category in a corner, so that you can pass the category to the spy without any fuss. A little organisation keeps the energy up.

Can you play it with the host's own words?

At a table with slips, yes. In the game I run, the words are dealt by the game, which is why you do not need a list to start. Use these lists for offline evenings and as inspiration.

Where can you play online?

At Board It, a free site I make, Who's the Spy runs as a spy game online for three to eight friends, or up to twelve round one phone. The imposter game suits a dinner party or a call equally well.
