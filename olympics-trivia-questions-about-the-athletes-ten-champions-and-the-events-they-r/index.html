Olympics trivia questions about the athletes: ten champions and the events they ruled

Ahmed Moaz · 4 November 2026

Everyone remembers a few Olympic names, and fewer remember the event that made each one famous. These Olympics trivia questions put the two together: a champion in the clue, an event or a number in the answer. They run from the 1936 Games to Paris 2024 and are written for a table that watches the Games every four years but does not read the record books.

The ten questions

  1. Which American sprinter won four gold medals at the 1936 Berlin Games?
  2. Which Romanian gymnast scored the first perfect 10 in Olympic history, in 1976?
  3. Which British runner won both the 5,000 m and the 10,000 m at the 2012 and 2016 Games?
  4. Which Swedish pole vaulter, nicknamed "Mondo", keeps breaking the world record?
  5. Which British athlete won the heptathlon at London 2012?
  6. In which sport did Sir Chris Hoy win six Olympic golds?
  7. Which American won nine Olympic golds in sprinting and the long jump?
  8. How many gold medals did Usain Bolt win at the Olympics?
  9. In which athletics event would you see the "Fosbury Flop"?
  10. Which American swimmer holds the record for the most Olympic golds?

The answers, with the detail behind each

  1. Jesse Owens. His wins in Berlin undermined the Nazi claim of racial superiority.
  2. Nadia Comăneci. She was 14, and the scoreboard showed 1.00 because it could not display a 10.
  3. Mo Farah. His "Mobot" celebration became famous worldwide.
  4. Armand Duplantis. He was born in the United States to an American father and a Swedish mother.
  5. Jessica Ennis-Hill. Her win was part of Britain's "Super Saturday".
  6. Track cycling. Hoy was Britain's most successful Olympian until Jason Kenny passed him.
  7. Carl Lewis. He won the long jump at four Games in a row.
  8. Eight. Bolt had a ninth taken away when a relay teammate failed a drugs test.
  9. The high jump. Dick Fosbury won gold with his backwards technique in 1968.
  10. Michael Phelps, with 23. At Beijing 2008 he won eight golds, one of them by 0.01 seconds in the 100 m butterfly.

Which questions will trip people up?

Questions 1, 2 and 10 are the ones most people can answer, since the names are famous even to people who do not follow athletics. Questions 4 and 5 are about recent champions, and I would expect a quiet room: people remember the face but not the name.

Question 8 hides a trap. Some players will say nine, because Bolt was once credited with nine, and the correction in the note is the part they enjoy. If you want a clean ruling, say "after the later disqualification" when you read it out.

Question 9 asks for an event, not a person, which reverses the pattern of the rest. I put it near the end so the table has settled into the habit of answering with a name.

Can question 10 be your tie-break?

Phelps's total is a good closest-number question. Ask "How many Olympic golds did he win in his career?" and let everyone write a number. The answer is 23, and a guess a little over or under still scores well. A number question is also how each round of the game below ends, and it settles ties without a long debate.

The thread running through all ten

These champions all have an event they are known for, and that is the useful pattern for writing your own questions. Pick a person, name the event in the question or the answer, and add one detail that is not on the medal table. The detail is what people repeat afterwards.
