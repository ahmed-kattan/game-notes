Online games for the classroom: five ideas for the last day of term

Ahmed Moaz · 23 October 2026

The last day of term has its own physics. Attention drains, desks empty and the timetable becomes a polite fiction. Teachers who want to keep the room together need games that start in two minutes and fill the lesson without a worksheet. These online games for the classroom run in a browser, need no accounts for guests and suit groups of different sizes. They come from a free site I make, Boardit, so the seat limits and lengths are exact.

What do you need?

A device per group, a link and a projector if you have one. Nothing else. Games can be played by pairs, small groups or a whole class split into tables.

1. A class quiz

Trivia Tavern seats up to eight per table and lasts about fourteen minutes. Pick a category, share the link and let the students answer on their own devices. For a class of thirty, run four tables and compare scores.

2. A drawing game

Draw and Guess seats up to twelve and lasts about twenty minutes. It needs no reading and rewards quick thinking, which suits the last day. Ask students to add words from the term's lessons, if the teacher sets up the table with custom words.

3. A clue game

Who's the Spy takes three to eight players online, or up to twelve around one phone. It trains listening and inference and takes about twenty minutes. Nobody is eliminated, so nobody sits out.

4. A race game

Snakes and Ladders seats up to eight and takes ten to twenty-five minutes. It is a game of chance, so every student has the same prospects, and it makes a gentle break after a hard term.

5. A strategy game for the keen

Ludo for up to four and a property game for up to six. Both take longer than a lesson slot, so agree a stopping rule.

How do you link the games to learning?

Ask a question after each game. What did the quiz show about what you remember? How did you decide who to trust in the clue game? Which drawing was clearest, and why? A five-minute talk after the fun turns a free lesson into a useful one. For younger classes, ask each group to tell one thing they enjoyed and one thing they would change.

A game with a score also gives you a natural way to praise good teamwork: give a point for the table that helped a neighbour, as well as for the strongest answers.

How do you keep order?

Use the game's own clock. A table with a thirty-second turn limit keeps the pace without a teacher shouting. Appoint a table captain who reports the scores, and make a visible scoreboard on the board.

How do you include everyone?

Mix the groups so that friends are not always together. Give quiet students roles, such as scorekeeper or question reader. A game with no elimination, such as the clue game, keeps everybody in till the bell.

What about devices?

Phones, tablets and laptops all work. If a group has only one device, they can share it. If a student has none, pair them with a friend. Browser games avoid the download problem that ruins school computer rooms.

Where can you set it up?

Board It is free and needs no accounts for guests, so a teacher can be playing online board games and party games online with a class in two minutes. It is a handy fallback for a lesson that has run out of material.

A last word for teachers

Keep the games short and the rules simple. A class that ends the term laughing will remember it kindly.
