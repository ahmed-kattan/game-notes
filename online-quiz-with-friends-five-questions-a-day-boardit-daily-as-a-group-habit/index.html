Online quiz with friends, five questions a day: Boardit Daily as a group habit

Ahmed Moaz · 5 November 2026

An online quiz with friends usually needs a booking: a time, a link, a host and a free evening. Most weeks nobody has all four. Boardit Daily is my answer to that. It is five trivia questions a day, the same five for everyone, and it takes a few minutes. It works as a group habit because it asks for so little.

What is in a Daily?

Every day has one set of five questions. They run from easy to harder, each with 20 seconds on the clock, and they are drawn from every category. You play it once. The set is the same for everyone in the world, so someone in another country faces the same five as you.

The day is counted in UTC. That means the new set arrives at midnight UTC, which falls at a different clock time for each of you. The page shows how long you have until the next one, so nobody has to calculate it.

Why does a shared set make a good habit?

Because there is nothing to organise. A group match needs a host and about 14 minutes with up to eight people at once. The Daily needs one person, once, whenever they like. Your friends can play on the train, between meetings or in bed, and still be in the same quiz.

It also removes the spoiler problem. Everyone has the same questions, so the talk afterwards is real. "Did you get the fourth one?" is a better opening than "how was your day".

What do you post to the group chat?

When you finish, the page gives you a short result to paste. It shows the date, one coloured square for each question, green for right and red for wrong, then your score out of five and your points. If you are on a run of more than one day, it adds your streak. A line at the bottom points back to the site.

What it leaves out matters just as much. It carries no answers, no name and no room link. A friend who has not played yet can read the squares in the chat without learning anything about the questions. I wrote it that way on purpose, so that sharing a result never spoils the next person's go.

How does the streak keep people coming back?

A streak counts Dailies finished on consecutive days. It does not break at midnight. If you haven't played today yet, yesterday's run still counts, and it is lost only when a whole day goes by with no Daily. That is gentler than a strict clock, and it suits a group where someone is always travelling.

A fair house rule is to let the streaks tell the story, not a points table. One friend on a 12-day run is a better prompt for a message than a score of 740.

How do you set up a Daily habit?

Keep it small. I would use three rules:

  • everyone posts their squares, and nobody posts an answer until the group agrees;
  • no one is allowed to ask for a replay, since the set is played once;
  • the person with the longest streak picks the category for the next group match.

The third rule links the Daily to the longer game. Boardit's Trivia Tavern holds both, so the same group can run the five-question Daily on weekdays and a full match once a week. A match runs about 14 minutes, and anyone who skipped the Daily can still join in.

When does it stop being fun?

When it becomes an obligation. If someone misses three days in a row and feels bad about it, the habit has stopped serving the group. The streak is gentle for a reason. It is a nudge, and it should never become a debt.

For a small group of friends, five questions a day is enough to keep an online trivia quiz alive between the big evenings, and it costs nobody a booking.
