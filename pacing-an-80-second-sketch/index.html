Pacing an 80-second sketch

Ahmed Moaz · 13 October 2026

With Draw and Guess, a good 80-second sketch splits into 10 seconds of outline, 40 of features and 30 of following the chat. The split follows the score: a guess pays 100 plus 5 points for every second left on the default clock, so the first ten seconds are worth the most and the last thirty the least. Here is the split second by second, and a worked example of what it is worth to a drawer in a drawing game online.

The 80 seconds, split three ways

Before the clock starts you have up to 12 seconds to choose. The drawing clock begins only once you have chosen, so use that time to find the single shape that signals the word.

  1. Seconds 0 to 10: the outline. One big shape that tells a stranger the category. No detail.
  2. Seconds 10 to 50: the features. Add the two or three marks that tell this object from its neighbours. The first hint lands 27 seconds in, so a reader should already have a pattern to match.
  3. Seconds 50 to 80: follow the chat. The second hint lands at 53 seconds. Read what people are typing, and add only what moves their guesses closer.

The chat is useful because wrong guesses are shown to the whole table, drawer included. If a guess names something the same shape, the outline worked, and the next mark should separate the two.

What the split is worth

Say five people are at the table, so four are guessing. The drawer is paid the speed points of every right answer, summed and shared across the four other players. Nobody guessing pays nothing.

Take two drawers who finish with the same picture:

  • Drawer A's outline lands early. Three guesses arrive 15, 25 and 40 seconds in, with 65, 55 and 40 seconds left. Those are worth 425, 375 and 300 points of speed, 1,100 in all. Divided by four, the drawer earns 275.
  • Drawer B spends the first 40 seconds on detail. The same three guessers arrive at 50, 60 and 70 seconds in, worth 250, 200 and 150, or 600 in all. The drawer earns 150.

Same word, same people, 125 points apart. The difference is when the table could read the drawing, and that is the early part of the clock, which the outline controls.

If nothing happens by the 40 second mark

A blank page is the worst case. If a drawer lets the clock run with nothing on the page twice in a row, the game takes them off the table. So an ugly outline at second 8 is better than a clean one at second 45. And if the chat has no sensible guess by 40 seconds, change the idea instead of adding detail: a second outline at a different angle can still land with 40 seconds left, worth 300 to the first guesser.

Practise it without a screen

For a real table, the clock and hint settings sit in the lobby you reach from playboardit.com/play/draw-and-guess.

Or start on paper with a kitchen timer: ten seconds, a pause, forty, a pause, thirty. Draw the same object three times. Then try it at a table with friends and watch whose outline gets read first. A 60-second clock moves the split to roughly 8, 30 and 22.
