Playing monopoly online free when your friends are busy: a table of you and the bots

Ahmed Moaz · 1 November 2026

Sooner or later you want a game of buying cities and charging rent, and nobody is free. Friends have work, family or a bad connection, and a property game needs more than one person. If you searched for monopoly online free because you want to play tonight, there is a way to do it alone. In Property Rush, the host can fill empty seats with bots and start at once. Here is how that works, and what it is good for.

Can you really play a property trading game alone?

Yes, in a sense. A property trading game needs rivals to charge rent to, and bots are rivals. The table seats two to six. A table of you and one bot is a full game, and you can add bots until the table has six seats, which means up to five of them.

Bots are named as bots, so nobody is fooled. They roll, buy cities, build, bid in auctions and offer you deals. You can accept a deal or turn it down, just as you would with a person. They play to win, which makes them more useful than a pushover.

How do you set one up?

Open the Property Rush page, create a table and stay in the lobby. Boardit is where I host it, and the host can fill free seats with bots before pressing start.

Choose the purse first. With bots at the table, I would start with the smallest one, $500, to learn the rules quickly. The default is $1,500, and the options go up to $5,000 for a long evening. The game has no cap on rounds, so the purse is the main way to set its length. The listed time for a table is forty-five minutes.

What is it good for?

Three things, mainly.

  • Learning. Rent, auctions, mortgages and building are easier to understand when nobody is waiting on you.
  • Practice. If you plan to host friends later, you can try the rule switches on your own and see what each one changes.
  • Filling a gap. If one friend cancels an hour before the game, a bot can take the empty seat instead of cancelling the evening.

That last one matters more than it sounds. Hosts often give up on a game because the table feels too small. A bot sitting in the fourth seat solves it in a few seconds.

What happens if a real person turns up?

If every free seat holds a bot, a person who joins before the start takes a bot's seat. So you can open a table, add bots, and still let a friend drop in later.

What can a bot not do?

Be honest about the limits. A bot does not haggle over a trade the way a friend does, and it will not tease you when you land on its hotel. A table of bots teaches you the rules. It does not teach you how your friends behave.

I also do not want to oversell the idea of a free evening with software. If your goal is a social night, you need people. If your goal is to try the game, the bots are enough.

Do bots appear elsewhere in the game?

Bots also stand in for people who go missing. A player who sits out two turns in a row is replaced by a bot until they return, so a dropped connection does not end the table. This works in Property Rush, Ludo and Snakes and Ladders. The other three games, Trivia Tavern, Who's the Spy and Draw and Guess, have no bots.

A quick plan for tonight

  1. Create a Property Rush table and add two or three bots.
  2. Set the purse to $500 for a short game.
  3. Keep auctions on, so you see how bidding works.
  4. Play to the end, then try the same table with a bigger purse.

Property Rush is an independent Monopoly alternative, so the board is its own, with twenty-two cities in eight countries. When your friends are free again, send them the code and play with friends. The bots will have taught you the rules by then.
