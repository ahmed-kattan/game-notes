Property trading game rules on one page: Property Rush from setup to the last bankruptcy

Ahmed Moaz · 8 November 2026

If you are about to play a property trading game with people who have never seen it, a single page of rules is worth more than a long manual. This is the whole rule set of Property Rush, an independent Monopoly alternative I build, in the order you need it. It uses the default switches; the host can change several.

Setup

Two to six players. Each starts on the Start tile with 1,500 in cash. The board has forty tiles: twenty-two cities in eight countries, four airports, two utilities, two card tiles (Treasure and Surprise) and the corners Start, Prison, Vacation and Go to Prison. Turn order is shuffled at the beginning.

A turn

Throw two dice and move that many tiles clockwise. If you throw doubles, throw again. Three doubles in a row send you to Prison. What you do depends on where you land.

Landing on a city

If nobody owns it, you may buy it for its price. If you decline, it goes to auction and the highest bid wins. If somebody owns it, you pay rent. If you own it, nothing happens.

Rent and sets

Each city has a base rent. If one player owns every city in a country, the rent on each doubles, even with no buildings. With the whole country, you may build houses, up to four on a city, then a hotel. Rent climbs steeply with each house. Houses must go up evenly across a country.

Airports and utilities

Airports cost 200. The rent is 25, 50, 100 or 200 depending on how many of the four you own. Utilities cost 150. The rent is four times the dice roll with one utility, ten times with both.

Cards and Start

Treasure and Surprise are card decks. Cards may pay you, charge you, move your token or send you to Prison. Passing Start pays you a bonus each lap, which is what funds the first houses.

Prison and Vacation

Three doubles in a row, the Go to Prison tile and some cards send you to Prison. You leave by throwing doubles, paying a fee, using a pardon card or after three turns. Leaving costs your turn by default. The Vacation corner pays out the fees collected, by default.

Money problems

If you cannot pay a rent, the rest becomes a debt. You clear it by mortgaging a city for half its price, selling buildings, or trading. While you owe, you cannot throw. You leave the game by declaring bankruptcy or walking away.

Mortgages

Mortgaging raises half a city's price. Lifting the mortgage costs that amount plus 10%. A mortgaged city collects no rent, and you cannot build on a group with a mortgaged city.

Trading

At any time you can propose a trade with another player: cities and cash. Both players must agree.

Winning

With no round limit, the last player left in wins. If the host sets one, the richest player at the end wins, counting cash, cities and buildings.

Timing

Each turn has a minute, then a personal three-minute bank that has to last the whole game. When both run out, a bot finishes the turn safely, so nobody can hold up the table and nobody is thrown out.

Where can you play?

You can play it on Board It, a free table for a property trading game online with friends, with two to six seats and no download. The table applies every rule above for you.

What to remember

Buy cities, complete countries, build early, keep some cash. Everything else is detail.

What do beginners get wrong?

Three things come up again and again. They buy every city and have no money to build. They forget that a set doubles the rent only if they own every city of it. And they treat Prison as a punishment, when it can be a place to wait out a dangerous board. If you are teaching someone, mention these three early.

The first game is the hardest, and a patient explanation is worth more than a perfect one. Play a few laps together, pausing to say what each tile does, and you will find that the rules settle into habit within an hour.
