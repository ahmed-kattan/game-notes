Team games for 8 people in 20 minutes: how seat limits shape a Friday slot

Ahmed Moaz · 17 October 2026

Choosing team games for 8 people looks like a question of taste. It is mostly arithmetic. A Friday afternoon slot for a remote team is twenty minutes, joining eats a few of them, and every game spends the rest differently. Three of the six free online games I build seat eight in one room. This piece compares two of these multiplayer browser games: Trivia Tavern, a quiz for two to eight players, and Draw and Guess, a drawing game for two to twelve. They fill the same slot in opposite ways, and the reason is the seat count.

How long does an online quiz take with eight?

In the quiz, everyone answers the same question at once on their own phone. The game card estimates 14 minutes for the default match, and that figure does not move with headcount. Eight players answer the same number of questions as two.

What changes is who sets the pace. A question closes the moment every player has locked in, so a table of eight waits for its slowest member each time. That is never longer than the answer clock, but a bigger table gains less from questions closing early.

The drawing game: every extra player adds a turn

The drawing game works the other way. The pencil goes round the table in seat order, so eight players make eight drawings before anyone draws twice. The card's 20 minutes assumes six players, three rounds and 80 seconds a drawing, with most turns ending early because everyone has guessed.

Scale that by headcount and eight players need about 27 minutes. That is my arithmetic on the card's own estimate, 20 × 8 ÷ 6, not a timed game.

The ceiling is longer still. Each turn also has a short word pick before the drawing and a 6-second result after it. With the 80-second clock, eight players over three rounds make 24 turns of up to 98 seconds each, about 39 minutes if nobody ever guessed early.

Team games for 8 people: a twenty-minute plan

  • Minutes 0 to 4: one person opens a private room and shares the code in the team chat. Everyone joins with a first name.
  • Minutes 4 to 18: the quiz, on its default match.
  • Minutes 18 to 20: read out the winner and stop, even if someone wants a rematch. Next Friday exists.

Both games work as quick team building with no facilitator, but only the quiz fits this slot for eight on its default settings. Give the drawing game a longer slot, about 35 minutes with joining, and save it for a week when you want people laughing at each other's sketches.

When the team is nine or more

At nine to twelve, the drawing game is the only one of the six that seats everyone in one room. By the same scaling, 20 × 12 ÷ 6, twelve players need about 40 minutes before anyone has even joined.

The quiz splits instead, and this is how to split a large team: twelve people become two rooms of six, each with its own questions and its own scores. There is no combined leaderboard, so compare the two winners out loud. Past twelve, both games need separate rooms.

All six games are on one page of a free game site I make called Board It.

What the estimates leave out

The drawing-game figures are my scaling of the card's estimate and a ceiling worked out from the clocks, not timed games. Joining is the one step nobody has timed. The game cards themselves are estimates, not maximums, so leave a few minutes spare the first time and trim the plan once you know your team.
