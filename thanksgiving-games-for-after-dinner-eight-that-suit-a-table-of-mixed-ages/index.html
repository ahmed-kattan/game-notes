Thanksgiving games for after dinner: eight that suit a table of mixed ages

Ahmed Moaz · 2 November 2026

The plates are cleared, the football is on in the next room, and someone asks what to do now. A few good Thanksgiving games answer that question before the sofa wins. Think of it as a short family game night for the hour after pie. Below are eight, split by what they need from the table. Four need only paper. Four use a phone or a laptop. The date in 2026 is Thursday 26 November, the fourth Thursday of the month.

Why does the day need games at all?

The day already holds a lot. US law fixed Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday of November in December 1941, and it has run on that day since 1942. Add the Detroit Lions game, which has been on every year since 1945, and the Macy's parade, which has run since 1924. Dinner has a long list of other events to compete with.

A game gives the table one thing to look at together. It also gives the quiet cousin and the loud uncle the same job. That is the whole case for planning one.

Four games that need only paper

Young children belong in this part of the evening. Boardit's terms start at age 13, so the little ones stay on paper.

  1. Thankful alphabet. Go around the table and name something you are thankful for, starting with A, then B, then C. The next person must repeat the earlier answers. It ends when the group gives up, usually near the middle of the alphabet.
  2. Letter and list. Pick a letter and three categories, such as foods, places and jobs. Everyone writes one answer per category in two minutes. An answer scores only if nobody else wrote it.
  3. Pass the drawing. Each person draws a turkey on a sheet, folds the top over and passes it on. The next person adds a body without looking. Unfold everything at the end.
  4. Recipe riddles. One person describes a family dish without naming it. The others guess. It is funniest when the dish is odd.

None of these needs a screen, a charger or a rulebook. I would run two of them at the children's end of the table while the adults finish their coffee.

Four games for phones and laptops

I make Boardit, which has six free party games online, and none needs a download.

  1. Snakes and Ladders takes up to eight players, and two is enough. It is listed at 10 to 25 minutes. It has almost no decisions, so a teenager and a grandparent are on even terms. The host can pick Classic, Ladder Garden with more ladders, or Snake Pit with more snakes. Turns have a 30-second clock, so nobody stalls the table.
  2. Who's the Spy suits a table that likes talking. Everyone gets a word except the spy, and each person types a clue of up to 20 characters. There are 25 themed word packs, and one of them is Celebrations & events. On one shared phone, up to 12 people can play together, so even the person without a device is in.
  3. Draw and Guess takes anywhere from two to twelve people. The host types in a private list of words, and the players never see it. Family words, like the dog's name or the cousin's old car, turns it into a private joke.
  4. Trivia Tavern is an online trivia game with a gentler pace you can switch on, for people who dislike a race. I would use it last, because it asks the most of the brain.

How do you choose which of the eight to play?

Count the people first, and the screens second. If the family is spread across homes, board games online with friends and cousins work over a video call too. With five people or fewer you can play almost anything. With ten, pick Draw and Guess, or Who's the Spy on one phone. Next, look at the oldest and youngest player. If either would struggle with a small screen, choose a paper game.

Then check the clock. Two short games feel better than one long one, and a game night that ends early leaves everyone wanting more. A 20-minute slot fits one Boardit game and a toast.

Last, ask who is tired. A cook who has been standing since morning will not enjoy a quiz. Put them on Snakes and Ladders, where luck does the work.

Leave the dishes for later

Pick two games, not eight, and keep them free of rules lawyers. Start with a paper game while people still sit at the table, then move to a screen game when the sofa fills. Keep the first one under 15 minutes. If the room is still happy, play again; if not, you stopped at the right time.
