The most valuable ladder is not the longest: 80 to 100 is worth 19.8 throws

Ahmed Moaz · 11 November 2026

Everyone thinks the longest ladder is the one to want. On the classic Snakes and Ladders board, it is not. I used a simulation of the rules to measure what each ladder is worth, by counting how many extra throws a game would take if it were removed.

The ladders

The board has nine ladders. Three stand out in my table.

LadderLandings per gameThrows added if removed
80 to 1000.4819.8
28 to 840.5810.4

The 28 to 84 ladder is the long one, a climb of 56 squares, and it is landed on more than half a time a game. Removing it adds about 10.4 throws.

The ladder from 80 to 100 climbs only 20 squares. It is landed on less often, 0.48 times a game. But it ends on the final square. Removing it adds nearly 20 throws, almost twice as much.

Why

A ladder to 100 does two jobs. It saves the squares between 80 and 100, and it also saves the wait for the exact number. The exact-finish rule costs about 3.4 throws a game, and a ladder that lands you on 100 skips that wait entirely. I think a ladder ending on the final square is worth more than its length.

Why the long one still matters

The 28 to 84 ladder is the biggest single jump on the board, so it changes a game more than any other ladder the moment you land on it. It is the ladder people remember. But the numbers say that remembering is not the same as measuring. The one that quietly saves the most throws is the short ladder at the very end.

What to take from it

  1. Square 80 is a lucky place to land. If you are close, aim for it.
  2. Ladders near the end are worth more than ladders near the start. They save the part of the board where the snakes sit.
  3. The long ladder helps, but it is not the whole game.

Ladders on the other boards

The classic board has a ladder that dominates, but the others do not. On Ladder Garden the most valuable ladder, 74 to 95, saves a counter 7.4 throws on average from its foot, and on Snake Pit the most valuable, 40 to 59, saves 8.5 throws. On a board of many ladders, no single one decides the game.

Caveat

These figures are for a single counter on the classic board, with one throw a turn and every extra rule off. I ran them on 17 October 2026. They describe the rules, not real games.
