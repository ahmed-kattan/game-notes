Three tries for a six: the Ludo setting, and the odds behind it

Ahmed Moaz · 7 November 2026

Ludo has a famous problem. You cannot move until you throw a six, and sometimes that takes a long time. Boardit has a setting to soften it: three tries for a six.

The problem in numbers

A die has six faces, so a six turns up on one throw in every six. On average, you wait six throws to see your first one. Half of all players see it in four throws or fewer, and some wait much longer.

With every piece still in base, three throws in a row without a six (5/6 × 5/6 × 5/6) happen 57.9% of the time, which leaves 42.1% for getting out. In four throws it is 48.2%. In ten throws, one game in six still has no six at all.

What the setting does

With three tries for a six switched on, a player who has no piece out gets to throw up to three times in a turn, instead of once. The chance of a six in three throws is 42.1%, so about two turns in five now start a piece. Without it, one turn in six does.

The default is off, which is the traditional rule. A host can turn it on in the lobby.

When to choose it

I would turn it on for:

  1. a table of children, who do not enjoy waiting;
  2. a short evening, when people want to play rather than watch;
  3. a first game, to let everyone see the board.

I would leave it off for a table of experienced players, who treat the wait as part of the tension.

Why three, and what more would do

The chance of at least one six in n throws is 1 minus five-sixths to the power n. For three throws that is 42.1%. For six throws it is 66.5%, and for ten throws 83.8%. Three tries is a middle path: a lot more likely than one, without turning the start of the game into a formality.

What else is in the lobby

The host can also pick Quick Ludo, with two pieces each and a game of about five minutes, and decide whether a capture or a piece home earns an extra throw. The lobby shows the setting before the first throw.

You can set it up at Boardit's Ludo. Turns have a 30-second clock.

The odds are my own calculation from the rules, and they apply to a single player with no piece out.
