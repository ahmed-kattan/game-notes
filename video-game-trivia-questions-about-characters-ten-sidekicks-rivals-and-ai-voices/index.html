Video game trivia questions about characters: ten sidekicks, rivals and AI voices

Ahmed Moaz · 3 November 2026

Every game has a hero, and every hero has people around them. This set of video game trivia questions is about those people: the flying fox, the ghost, the horse and the voice in the walls. Each one has a clear answer, a short note, and a source page listed in the front matter. They need no screen and no controller, only a good memory, and the list suits a family trivia night as much as a gamers' meetup.

Ten questions about the cast

  1. Which flying fox is Sonic's closest friend?
  2. Who is Sonic's moustached arch-enemy, a scientist?
  3. Who is the red echidna who starts as Sonic's rival?
  4. Who is Mario's greedy rival in yellow?
  5. Which ghost enemies in Mario games cover their faces when you look at them?
  6. Link's fairy companion in Ocarina of Time has which name?
  7. Link's horse has which name?
  8. Which evil AI controls the test chambers in Portal?
  9. Master Chief's AI companion in Halo has which name?
  10. Which Pokémon has eight possible evolutions?

The answers

  1. Tails. He can fly by spinning his two tails like a helicopter.
  2. Dr. Eggman. In the West he was first called Dr. Robotnik.
  3. Knuckles, who guards the Master Emerald on Angel Island.
  4. Wario. His name mixes "Mario" with "warui", the Japanese word for bad.
  5. The Boos. One story says a designer's wife inspired them, because she was shy.
  6. Navi. Her constant calls for attention became a running joke among players.
  7. Epona, named after a Celtic goddess of horses.
  8. GLaDOS.
  9. Cortana. Microsoft later named its voice assistant after her.
  10. Eevee, whose evolutions fans call the "Eeveelutions".

Which questions split a table?

Number 2 is a quiet favourite. Players may know him as Robotnik or as Eggman, depending on which games they grew up with. The answer covers both names, so give the point either way and read out the note.

Number 9 has a second life outside the game. Cortana is also the name of a software assistant, which is a fun fact to share while you check the next question.

The Zelda pair, 6 and 7, is friendly to anyone who has played a Zelda game, and a mystery to everyone else. In a room of mixed ages, read them back to back so players can hear the link between them.

What if only a few players know games?

A character trivia quiz can feel closed to people who do not play, but it need not be.

Before each reveal, invite guesses for a rival, a sidekick or a robot. Players who have never touched a console can still answer number 4, because "greedy rival in yellow" is a clue in itself. I wrote the question text so that it helps a guesser as well as rewarding a fan, which keeps the round open to anyone with general knowledge and a little nerve.

Is there a simple way to run this with friends online?

Boardit's Trivia Tavern takes between two and eight players, and its listing puts a full game at roughly 14 minutes, so a character round fits inside a lunch break. Every person answers on a screen of their own, and the game can use the Video games category or a general mix. It suits friends who want to play trivia online and talk about games, not argue over a map.

How these were checked

The video games file in my question set supplied all ten, and I read the list through once more on 27 October. Where a story has a few versions, such as the origin of the Boos, I write "one story says" and not a flat fact.
