Who's the Spy with two spies: how the numbers change at a table of six

Ahmed Moaz · 12 November 2026

The host of a Who's the Spy table can choose how many spies there are, up to half the table. With six players, that means up to three. Two spies change the game more than you might think.

The blind-vote numbers

If a civilian knew nothing and voted for a random other player, the chance of naming a spy depends on how many spies there are. At a table of six, a civilian chooses among five others.

SpiesChance a random vote names a spy
120% (1 in 5)
240% (2 in 5)
360% (3 in 5), but a spy majority is too strong

With two spies, a random vote is twice as likely to hit one. A vote gives a point for naming a spy, so civilians score more easily, and the spies score less often by escaping.

But the clues are harder to read

With one spy, a clue that does not fit points to one person. With two, the clues that do not fit may come from two different directions, and the table has to decide whether it is looking at one confused civilian or two spies.

What the spies get

Two spies are on the same side and know the category. They cannot share the word, because they do not have it. They help each other only by giving clues that fit together, which looks suspicious if it is too neat.

How the points go

You score 1 for voting for a spy. A spy scores 1 if nobody votes for them. A lone spy who names the word scores 1 more. With two spies, neither is a lone spy, so that last bonus is not available in the same way.

When to use two

  1. A table of six or more. There are enough civilians to hold a vote.
  2. A group that knows the game. A second spy makes the round harder.
  3. A longer evening. It adds variety to the second half.

Where to play

Boardit's Who's the Spy is free, for three to eight players online. That is the imposter game, played with a secret word.

How many must ask

With two spies at a table of six there are 15 possible pairs of spies, and four of the six players must ask before a vote opens, which is more than half. The spies can protect each other, but they cannot stop a vote that four players want.

The numbers in a line

Two spies double a random vote's chance, and double the confusion.
